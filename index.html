The Matrix approach

Matrix begins before the product.

The customer’s operation is the starting point. We examine the work, the outcome, the requirements, and the constraints before deciding what should be replaced, retained, connected, or supported differently.

Matrix designs the environment, implements it, supports it, and helps adapt it as the work and technology change.

Work → Requirements → Design → Technology

A balanced operating decision

There is no single measure of the right environment.

Only your operation tells us what must be balanced.

Continuity, economics, security and control, usability, workflow, internal capability, external support, technology requirements, and operating constraints all affect the decision.

Matrix helps make those relationships visible so the environment fits the people doing the work—not a predetermined product or service model.

01

Understand the work.

Clarify what people need to accomplish, what must continue, and where friction or dependency affects the operation.

02

Define and balance the outcome.

Consider the requirements and tradeoffs together rather than optimizing one measure at the expense of the operation.

03

Design before selecting technology.

Determine the operating and support structure first. Then select technology that enables it.

The product is part of the answer. The operating environment is the decision.

Information responsibility

Intellectual property is today’s modern currency.

Every organization holds information that creates value or carries responsibility. It may be customer information, financial records, patient information, engineering drawings, legal documents, credentials, operating procedures, or institutional knowledge.

That information does not stop requiring protection when it leaves the screen.

When information is printed, scanned, copied, transmitted, stored, or serviced, the output system becomes part of its chain of custody. Matrix helps organizations examine that boundary as operational infrastructure, not merely office equipment.

Accountability over time

Matrix remains responsible for the environment it helps establish.

Responsibility does not mean Matrix must perform every action or guarantee every result. It means we remain accountable for the design, implementation, support structure, recovery boundaries, and continued fit of the recommendation.

The environment may appropriately use customer capability, Matrix capability, manufacturer resources, specialized expertise, automation, and defined escalation. As the work and requirements change, Matrix helps reconsider the model rather than waiting only for failure or replacement.

INPOWER®

A specific operating model for output recovery.
